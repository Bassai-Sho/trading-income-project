# PR-005 Pre-registration — DRAFT v0.1 (NOT FROZEN)

*28 Sep 2026. Status: draft, for red-team review before freeze. Exists to satisfy the pre-registration this project committed to before PR-004's mechanism test runs (PR-003/PR-004 record, 28 Sep 2026): the weeks-to-months "swing" instinct gets tested here, on its own terms, decided before PR-004 unblinds — not as a fallback chosen after seeing PR-004's result. **Nothing below has been run.***

## 0. Why this item, and what it is not

PR-004 tests a ~3–4 day mean-reversion rule. The owner's original description was "weeks or months" — that is trend-following territory, not PR-004's family, and this project's own record says so plainly: whatever PR-004 shows is evidence about one days-scale rule, not about "swing trading" as a family.

**This is not a full replication of the published evidence base.** The strongest published results for this idea (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, "Time Series Momentum," *Journal of Financial Economics*, 2012 — cross-asset momentum across equities, bonds, currencies, commodities) and Faber's own extensions (2007/2013 — a 13-asset-class tactical allocation) get their diversification benefit from **combining many low-correlation instruments**. This project holds data for four US-equity-family tickers (SPY, QQQ, NVDA, TSLA) plus SPY-linked series — no bonds, commodities, or currencies. **A genuine cross-asset test is out of scope here** and would need new data acquisition, which is a separate, explicit decision, not something to smuggle in later as a "descriptive extension." What *is* testable with data already in hand is a **single-asset trend-timing overlay** — does a slow, mechanical rule reduce the drawdown of the SPY exposure the owner already holds, without abandoning the upside. That is still decision-relevant to owner goal (2), and it is a real, if narrower, test.

## 1. Question and role

Owner goals (recorded 28 Sep 2026): (1) income/wealth, (3) learn/build capability, with (2) diversification/drawdown-reduction secondary. This item is aimed squarely at (2): can a slow, published, unoptimised timing rule reduce the drawdown the owner is already exposed to via a long equity portfolio, at low turnover and low cost-sensitivity (the opposite trade-off from PR-004's high-frequency signal)? Time-box: within the existing 21 Nov 2026 review; effort estimate in §11.

**Stated prior:** Faber's original published result (S&P 500, 1901 onward) reports a modest return improvement but a **large drawdown reduction** versus buy-and-hold, with a win rate around 55% and winning trades roughly six times the size of losing ones (asymmetric payoff, not a high hit rate). If this replicates, the expected outcome is "similar or slightly lower return, meaningfully smaller drawdown" — not an income source. That is the honest bar to test against, stated now.

## 2. Hypotheses

**H0 — replication.** The rule, as specified in §3, reproduces the qualitative pattern Faber reports on the S&P 500 back to as far as this project's data reaches (1993, not 1901 — see §5): materially smaller max drawdown than buy-and-hold, comparable or modestly lower CAGR, at monthly turnover.

**H1 — value over simply holding.** Net of costs, the timed exposure improves risk-adjusted return (Sharpe) and/or reduces max drawdown versus buy-and-hold SPY, on the primary evaluation window (§5).

**H2 — the blend test (the owner's real decision).** Does adding this rule as a *partial replacement* for existing SPY exposure (not an addition to it, unlike PR-003/PR-004's sleeves) improve the risk profile of a portfolio that already holds SPY? Reported, not gated — this is the actual portfolio-level question, but it depends on allocation choices that are the owner's, not a pass/fail research question.

## 3. Rules

**Primary, T1 — Faber's 10-month SMA timing rule**, applied to SPY (the nearest available proxy for the S&P 500 total-return series Faber used; see §4 for the total-return handling):

- Monthly signal, computed on the last trading day of each month.
- **Long (hold SPY)** when month-end price > 10-month simple moving average of month-end prices.
- **Flat (hold cash)** when month-end price < 10-month SMA.
- Ties (price exactly equal to the SMA, to a fixed tolerance): hold the current position (no unnecessary trade) — a convention, not in Faber's own text, stated here because it will otherwise be implementer discretion.
- Entry/exit executed at the **next trading day's open** after the signal (Stage V convention, matching PR-003/PR-004's discipline; Faber's own paper uses the signal-day close, which is Stage R here — see below).
- No stops, no leverage, single instrument. Position is either 100% SPY or 100% cash — never short.

**Two execution stages**, matching this project's standing pattern: **Stage R (fidelity)** — trade at the signal day's close, as Faber's paper does. **Stage V (viability)** — trade at the next trading day's open, with costs.

**Declared secondary variants** (each counts as a trial): **V2** — 200-day SMA on daily data instead of 10-month SMA on monthly data (a faster, more common retail version of the same idea, and testable at daily rather than monthly resolution). **V3** — the same rule applied to QQQ instead of SPY.

**Excluded, to control multiplicity:** any multi-asset or cross-sectional-momentum variant (out of scope per §0); any SMA length other than 10-month/200-day; any leverage or short extension; any volatility-targeting overlay; any combination with PR-003 or PR-004.

## 4. Data and integrity (gate G0)

**Primary:** SPY daily open/high/low/close, unadjusted, plus dividends, yfinance, 1993-01-29 to 2024-12-31 (already verified available: 8,038 daily rows, 129 dividend rows). Resampled to month-end for the primary rule. **Total-return handling:** Faber's own series are total-return (dividends reinvested); SPY's price series is not. The primary test therefore uses a total-return reconstruction (price return plus reinvested dividends, monthly), stated explicitly rather than silently mixing conventions — a price-only sensitivity is reported alongside it, not gated.

**Loader requirement (carried from PR-004's own bug fix):** yfinance's `end` parameter is exclusive; request `end = intended_end + 1 day` and assert the last row matches. **Calendar:** month-end signal dates taken from `exchange_calendars` XNYS (already a working dependency in this repo), not from yfinance's own last-row-per-month, to avoid a circular definition of "month-end."

**Asserts (reused from PR-004's G0):** `high ≥ max(open, close, low)`, `low ≤ min(open, close, high)`, `volume ≥ 0`; no duplicate dates; `Close` (never `Adj Close`) used for the price series, with dividends added back explicitly for the total-return reconstruction; ≥ 10 months of prior data before every evaluation month; |daily return| ≤ 15% sanity bound.

**Honest disclosure, matching PR-004's own corrected practice:** the analysts already know 2008–2022 market history in outline (the 2008–09 crisis, the 2020 COVID crash, the 2022 drawdown) — that cannot be un-known. What is genuinely new evidence is E4 and, later, forward paper trading.

## 6. Costs

**[ASSUMED — for review]** Monthly rebalancing means few round trips a year (a handful, not the ~4/year of PR-004's rule, and far below PR-003's intraday frequency), so cost-sensitivity is expected to be low — that is itself part of what this item tests, not assumed away. Execution: 5 bps of notional per side (spread plus slippage on a single monthly trade, generous relative to SPY's typical liquidity). Cash return while flat: the daily effective fed funds rate (DFF, FRED — already used in PR-003/PR-004), not zero — unlike Faber's own 90-day commercial paper proxy, stated as a deliberate, disclosed deviation. Dividends: included via the total-return reconstruction in §4 while long; none accrue while flat (correctly, since the position is in cash).

## 7. Gates (in order)

- **G0** Data integrity (§4). Failure stops the item.
- **G1 Implementation.** Two independently coded versions (one vectorised, one an explicit month-by-month loop) agree on 100% of position-months and monthly P&L, under the tie policy in §3.
- **G2 (H0), qualitative replication on E1+E2.** Max drawdown materially smaller than buy-and-hold's over the same window (threshold: **no worse than 60% of buy-and-hold's max drawdown** — a stated, round number, not fitted); CAGR within a band of buy-and-hold's (**−3 to +3 percentage points**, since Faber's own result is "comparable, not better"). Both bands **[ASSUMED — for review]**. Failure protocol, matching PR-004: one documented investigation (data first, then implementation), at most one fix-and-rerun; an unattributable miss closes the item.
- **G3 (H1), primary test on E3.** Net of Stage V costs: Sharpe ≥ buy-and-hold's Sharpe, **or** max drawdown ≤ 60% of buy-and-hold's — an "or," not an "and," because the rule's own evidence base predicts a drawdown-reduction story more than a return-improvement one, and gating on both would likely fail a working rule for the wrong reason (the same lesson PR-004's red-team review taught about exposure-diluted Sharpe hurdles). Both conditions reported regardless of which one gates.
- **G4 Economic report** (not gated): CAGR, vol, Sharpe, max drawdown, number of round trips, time invested vs. in cash, year-by-year table, the V2 (200-day) and V3 (QQQ) variants reported alongside, price-only vs. total-return sensitivity, and — the actual owner-relevant number — **the blend test (H2):** the risk profile of a portfolio holding X% timed-SPY and (100−X)% plain SPY, at X = 25/50/100%, versus 100% plain SPY.
- **G5 (E4, once):** single named, hashed script; report only, same discipline as PR-004.

**Verdict categories, matching PR-004's structure:** CONFIRMED (G2 and G3 both pass) / CONTRADICTED (G3's Sharpe *and* drawdown both come in worse than buy-and-hold, not just one) / INCONCLUSIVE (mixed). **Decision rule:** proceed to forward paper trading iff G0–G2 pass and the verdict is not CONTRADICTED. Given a monthly rule generates very few independent observations even over decades, this item is expected to be **power-limited by design** — that is disclosed here, not discovered later: a genuinely working rule can easily land in INCONCLUSIVE, and that is a legitimate, expected outcome, not a failure of the design.

## 8. Multiple testing and stop rules

Three declared trials (T1 primary, V2, V3); no DSR (matching PR-004's own reasoning — a single primary endpoint and a frozen, closed variant list do the relevant work at this trial count). The excluded-variants list in §3 may never be cited as grounds for adopting a new variant; any cross-asset extension is a new pre-registration, not a "sensitivity check" on this one.

## 9. Known weak points

1. **Low statistical power by construction** — monthly signals over even 15 years give roughly 15 independent-ish annual observations; this item is explicitly not powered to give a confident CONFIRMED/CONTRADICTED verdict, only a directional one, disclosed in §7.
2. **Single-instrument, not the cross-asset design the published evidence relies on** (§0) — the drawdown-reduction claim is the part of Faber's result most likely to survive a single-instrument test (it's mostly about exiting *this* asset's own crashes); the return-improvement claim is the part most likely not to.
3. **E1 cannot replicate Faber's exact published figures** (data starts in 1993, not 1901) — only the qualitative pattern is checked.
4. **The 2008–2022 market narrative is not new information to the analysts** — disclosed in §5, same as PR-004's corrected practice.
5. **Same-author risk on the two G1 implementations** — mitigated the same way as PR-004: an independent manual spot-check of several transition months by the owner.
6. **Total-return reconstruction from price + dividends is an approximation of a true total-return index** — small tracking differences vs. an actual total-return series are possible and are disclosed in the report, not treated as a data error.

## 10. Relationship to PR-004

This item's design does not depend on PR-004's outcome in any way, and PR-004's freeze does not depend on this item completing — they proceed independently once both are frozen.
