## 0. What changed, and how each was verified

| # | Change | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CRITICAL — v0.3's analytic dominance benchmark was beatable by static mixes.** The linear formula `r_cash + ratio·(CAGR_BH − r_cash)` assumed a mix's drawdown ratio ≈ its weight *w*; I tested this directly on real E3 data and found the assumption doesn't hold cleanly in either direction claimed. My first simulation attempt (weighting price *levels*) was itself a different error, caught before it could mislead anything — a static level-weighted blend is a buy-once, never-rebalanced portfolio, not the same object the Sharpe-invariance proof assumed. Redone with the correct **daily-rebalanced** construction (`r_mix = w·r_SPY + (1−w)·r_cash`, matching the proof), I found the mix's own drawdown ratio consistently **exceeds** *w* (the opposite direction from what was proposed), yet the linear benchmark was still beatable in most cells, especially at higher cash rates — via compounding/volatility-drag interaction, not the mechanism originally described. Both the specific claimed direction and my own first attempt were wrong; the bottom-line vulnerability was real. | Two independent simulations on real, fetched E3 data (shown in this session): the buggy level-weighted version and the corrected daily-rebalanced version, across four plausible cash rates and seven mix weights each. |
| 2 | **Fixed with an empirical frontier, not another formula.** `F(ρ) := max{CAGR(w) : DD_ratio(w) ≤ ρ}`, computed directly from a fine grid of simulated mixes (201 points) rather than approximated analytically. Zero exceedances, as the construction requires. |

## 1. The dominance test, corrected

**G3 (H1):** compute the empirical frontier *F* from a fine grid of static SPY/cash mixes on E3 (real data, no strategy signal — the same computation shown in §0 item 2), using the real cash rate once pulled (§8, still pending — the grid above used illustrative rates only, and the qualitative finding, that a naive linear line is beatable, held across all of them, but the frozen frontier itself needs the real rate). The rule passes iff its realized Stage-V-net CAGR **strictly exceeds** *F* evaluated at its own realized Stage-V-net drawdown ratio. This is mathematically guaranteed to exclude every possible static mix, verified by direct computation rather than argued from an approximation.
